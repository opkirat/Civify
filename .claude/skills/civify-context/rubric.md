# Judging rubric (verbatim)

Total is 100 points: Project 40, Software 20, Presentation 40. Each criterion is scored 1 (Limited), 3 (Meets expectations) or 5 (Excellent), then scaled to its points.

## Part 1: Project (40 points)

### Problem understanding and stakeholders addressed (10 points)
- **1 · Limited:** The problem and affected stakeholders are unclear. Benefits or impacts are largely assumed. The solution does not address the needs of the primary stakeholders.
- **3 · Meets expectations:** Defines the problem and key stakeholders. Benefits and impacts are laid out and considers some differing needs or trade-offs. The solution only addresses the needs of the primary stakeholders.
- **5 · Excellent:** Clearly understands the problem and stakeholder needs beyond the surface level. Clearly shows expected benefits and explains potential harms and trade-offs. The solution addresses the needs of the primary stakeholders, plus additional stakeholders.

### Project quality and functionality (15 points)
- **1 · Limited:** The core task cannot be completed, or outputs have major errors. Key features are mainly simulated. There are existing technical flaws that are not addressed.
- **3 · Meets expectations:** The core task works on a relevant example. Main features are operational, some tertiary features are mocked up. Some gaps, errors or workarounds remain.
- **5 · Excellent:** The core workflow produces useful, checked outputs. Main features are fully operational with very minimal placeholder aspects. Results are reliable; data limits and incomplete features are clear. Responsible use of data and AI.

### Design, clarity and feasibility (10 points)
- **1 · Limited:** Design could not be implemented in a real-world setting without substantial rework to address the municipal context. A first-time user needs substantial help to navigate the tool or interpret its output.
- **3 · Meets expectations:** Design could be implemented with some tweaks for the municipal context. A first-time user can complete the main task with instructions, but encounters some confusing steps or terminology.
- **5 · Excellent:** Design could be implemented right away in a municipal context. A novice completes the main task independently, supported by clear navigation, plain language, accessible design and useful guidance.

### Potential impact and practical value (5 points)
- **1 · Limited:** The connection to the challenge statement is unclear; a useful application is not established. Design is not an improvement over what already exists or processes already in place. Solution is specific to only one example municipal use case.
- **3 · Meets expectations:** Clear connection to most aspects of the challenge statement. Connects the project to relevant priorities and identifies a plausible use and benefit for a host, sponsor or municipal partner. Design is an incremental improvement over what already might exist. Impact is strong but could be difficult to scale to use cases at other municipalities.
- **5 · Excellent:** Demonstrates strong alignment with the problem identified in the challenge, a credible organizational use and clear practical value. The potential impact is strong and would lead to substantial positive, real-world outcomes. The presented solution/tool could be scaled to other municipalities.

## Part 2: Software design, architecture and code understanding (20 points)

### Software requirements and design (UML) (5 points)
- **1 · Limited:** Requirements are unclear or incomplete. UML diagrams are missing, inaccurate, or inconsistent with the implementation.
- **3 · Meets expectations:** Defines essential functional and non-functional requirements. Relevant UML diagrams generally align with the implementation.
- **5 · Excellent:** Defines clear, complete, and testable requirements. Accurate UML diagrams show how the design addresses the requirements and align with the implementation.

### Software architecture rationale and scalability considerations (5 points)
- **1 · Limited:** Describes the architecture with little justification or connection to requirements. Provides little consideration of growth in users, data, or workload.
- **3 · Meets expectations:** Explains major architectural choices and connects them to requirements and relevant design principles. Identifies likely scalability challenges and explains suitable approaches to address them.
- **5 · Excellent:** Justifies architectural choices by comparing alternatives and evaluating trade-offs, including maintainability, testability, scalability, and requirements alignment. Analyzes expected growth and potential bottlenecks; justifies design choices and trade-offs using concrete scenarios or evidence.

### Source code awareness (5 points)
- **1 · Limited:** Cannot adequately explain key code, its behaviour, or its integration. Uses generated or copied code without demonstrating understanding or verification.
- **3 · Meets expectations:** Explains key code and demonstrates understanding of generated or adapted components. Verifies that the code works as intended.
- **5 · Excellent:** Clearly explains implementation choices, dependencies, and limitations. Demonstrates the ability to modify, debug, and validate generated or adapted code independently.

### Source code, framework, library and algorithm references (5 points)
- **1 · Limited:** References are missing, incomplete, or insufficient to identify reused sources.
- **3 · Meets expectations:** Identifies and cites external code, frameworks, libraries, and algorithms; discloses AI assistance where used.
- **5 · Excellent:** Provides complete, traceable references, including relevant versions and licences; clearly identifies adaptations and documents how AI assistance was used and verified.

## Part 3: Presentation (40 points)

### Municipal readiness report (20 points)
- **1 · Limited:** The report is difficult to follow; the rationale, evidence or recommendations are unclear or unsupported. Not all questions are fully answered.
- **3 · Meets expectations:** Explains the problem, approach and results for a municipal audience; includes sources and some limitations and next steps. All questions are addressed.
- **5 · Excellent:** A clear municipal readiness report that connects stakeholder needs, evidence and results to actionable recommendations and pilot next steps. Sources and limitations are explicit. Questions are addressed thoughtfully and completely.

### Demo video (20 points)
- **1 · Limited:** The video does not clearly explain the problem or show how the tool works; key claims are difficult to assess. No evidence of storytelling.
- **3 · Meets expectations:** Explains the problem and intended users and demonstrates the main feature; some steps, benefits or limitations need clarification.
- **5 · Excellent:** Clearly explains the problem, users and value. Demonstrates the core workflow and outputs; distinguishes working features from plans. Audio and visuals are understandable and well presented.
